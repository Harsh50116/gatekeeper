Good morning. In business, Apple reported its fiscal second quarter results yesterday, with revenue of ninety-four billion dollars, beating analyst estimates of ninety-one billion. CEO Tim Cook credited strong iPhone 16 demand in India and Southeast Asia. The company announced a one hundred and ten billion dollar share buyback, the largest in corporate history.

Meanwhile, Microsoft posted revenue of sixty-two billion dollars for the same quarter, slightly missing expectations of sixty-three billion. CEO Satya Nadella acknowledged slower-than-expected Azure growth but pointed to record enterprise AI adoption, with over sixty percent of Fortune 500 companies now using Azure OpenAI services.

In world news, Turkey and Greece signed a historic maritime boundary agreement after decades of disputes in the Aegean Sea. The deal, brokered by the European Union, includes joint exploration rights for natural gas reserves and a commitment to reduce military patrols in contested waters.

In science, NASA confirmed its Dragonfly mission to Saturn's moon Titan is on track for a twenty twenty-eight launch. The nuclear-powered rotorcraft will search for signs of prebiotic chemistry in Titan's methane lakes.
